Iron Rain · development logbook

After Paradise

A turn-based tactics game about a squad of mechs on Earth after “paradise”. The aliens came bearing a cure and left mutants behind. Here you can see what already works in the game, which missions are ready and what comes next.

Intro

How it all began: the intro before the first mission

The game opens with a black-and-white hand-drawn story of how “paradise” arrived and how it ended. It is told by Victor, the scientist who once opened the door to the aliens. The camera drifts across drawn pages, captions appear as you read, and the only colour is pink: that is how the aliens look in this world. The music is by the game’s author. Victor’s voice will be recorded by dubbing actors; for now the captions are read only.

Alpha version: no voice or sound effects, music only. About three minutes.

Campaign · Terra

Seven missions of the first planet

Every planet has seven missions, the seventh is a boss. Story missions alternate with simple battles: a simple battle is a breather that gives pilots experience and items from pods, but it always has a small goal of its own. The campaign starts with the Drill.

Save the Captain

The camera shows an old fighter jet downed in a swamp. Through the clear canopy you can see a living man: Terra Air Force captain Billy Big Bogs, one of the last resistance fighters. The mutants are already heading for him.

Goal: fight through to the plane, beat off the waves and escort the captain to evac under cover. As a reward he hands over his personal weapon: it hits weakly, but never runs out of ammo.

07
BossRules decided

Bellows

The former commander of a military airfield: short red hair, uniform jacket, boots. Instead of arms she has wide webbed wings, and a filter collar rings her head. She flies in for the civilians.

Goal: save as many people as you can and kill Bellows. Acid from her collar eats armour every turn, so the pods hold medkits.

Decided
  • Bellows flies up to a house and next turn inhales everyone inside through her collar, healing herself for each one. To stop her, push her away from the house.
  • Or a mech next to the house leads the people out, and they run to Iron Rain.
  • Every two people saved earn the squad a lab credit.
  • Each of the three pods holds a medkit: it removes acid, and on easy difficulty it also heals.
  • The 3×3 acid cloud is shown one turn before it hits. The cloud and the house hunt alternate.
Work log

What was done and when

Newest entries first. The game is made by a team of three. The author came up with all of it: the world and its story, the battle rules, missions, mutants and pilots. He also writes the music and sounds and plays every build. Codex draws, Claude writes the code. The squad will be voiced by professional dubbing actors. The art is made with AI: Codex draws from the author’s descriptions and sketches, and every image goes through sketches and the author’s choice.

8 NovSunday

Mean mutants, Knuckleback’s concrete and a thought about multiplayer

  • The author came back from vacation and played Vial. The main thing he and all the testers said: the enemies are sluggish. Measurements confirmed it — four turns out of five the mutants stood idle.
  • Now there are almost no idle turns. If a mutant can’t reach a mech, it hits a supply pod or a tile next to a mech, which makes those tiles dangerous to step on. Attacks in a crowd went up from 22% to 40% of turns.
  • Knuckleback learned to throw concrete 2–4 tiles. If you dodge, the chunk stays on the tile as rubble, and a single shot breaks it. Codex drew the chunk and the rubble.
  • Click a mutant and a red trail runs from it to its target: you can see where it will strike.
  • Overwatch prompts what the mission needs next: shotgun, ammo, vial, evac. The frame blinks red until the step is done.
  • In Vial, after the alarm the mutants come out right next to the evac zone, to make it hot there.
  • Tester builds moved to itch.io: a private page with automatic updates. Codex drew a cover: Bastion versus Knuckleback.
  • The author has an idea for multiplayer in later versions of the game. It won’t be in the first release, but the idea is written down.
  • Claude estimated how hard it is. For turn-based tactics, networking is one of the more feasible additions: the network carries short commands like “Bastion moves to C4”, not a picture sixty times a second.
  • The battle is already built in a convenient way: the rules are separate from the visuals, and randomness comes from the map seed. Two computers with the same seed and the same commands get the same battle.
  • The simplest step is two players at one computer. With Steam Remote Play Together you can then play with a friend in another city, and the friend doesn’t even need to buy the game.
  • Real online co-op would take a month or two of work; we’ll do it if players want to play together. The most interesting and the hardest option is one player on the mechs and the other on the mutants: the mutants announce their attacks, the squad responds.

Voices

The squad will speak with actors’ voices

The pilots, Overwatch and the captain will be voiced by professional dubbing actors: the people whose voices film heroes speak with in Russian dubs. This is not speech synthesis and not “friends doing voices” — every line will be recorded in a studio.

There are already more than a hundred lines: pilots bicker on the radio, Overwatch comments on every turn, mutants get their own “field notes”. For now we’re trying the texts out in the game, and we’ll record once they settle — so the actors read the final version once.

Sketchbook

Sketches and drafts

Everything drawn for the game goes through sketches: the author describes what an image should be, Codex draws several options, the author picks one. Here is what came out along the way, including what didn’t make it into the game.

Cover: Bastion versus Knuckleback

The author’s idea was like the strategy game covers of the nineties: two fighters from two sides face off. Codex drew two options in the game’s style, but the author picked the very first one — alive and not quite like the game: “not everything is recognisable, and that’s fine”. The cover greets testers on the game’s private page.
